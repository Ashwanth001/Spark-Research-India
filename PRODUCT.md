# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Students (Classes 8–12)**: Self-directed students who have heard about IRIS/ISEF or are curious about research and want to find out if SRI is right for them. They are evaluating the programme on their own terms — what they'll get, whether it's legit, how to sign up.

**Teachers & school administrators**: Educators who discover the programme and refer or enrol students, or partner with SRI as a school. Time-poor, sceptical of edtech marketing, responsive to credibility signals.

**Parents**: Families of Indian students deciding whether to invest. Concerned about value, legitimacy, and outcomes.

Context: Users may land on the website from a school presentation, social media, or word of mouth. The website must serve all three audiences without alienating any — students want to feel seen and excited; parents want to feel reassured; teachers want a clear partnership story.

Job to be done: get a motivated student (or their parent/teacher) from "interested" to "registered" — or to nominate for a scholarship.

## Product Purpose

Spark Research India guides Indian students (Classes 8–12) from a research idea to the IRIS National Fair — and on to ISEF, the world's largest pre-college science competition. It is the only mentorship programme built specifically for the IRIS → ISEF pathway, priced for Indian families (₹20–30k vs. ₹1.5–5 Lakh globally), with full scholarships for underprivileged students.

Success looks like: a teacher finishes the deck believing SRI is credible and worth sharing with a curious student in their class — and feeling confident nominating a student for a scholarship.

## Brand Personality

Inspiring · Accessible · Expert

Tone: confident and precise without being corporate; warm without being soft. The programme has real ambition (world stage) paired with genuine inclusion (full scholarships, India-first design). Neither element should overshadow the other.

## Anti-references

- **Edtech coaching-centre aesthetics** (BYJU's, Vedantu, WhiteHat Jr): busy layouts, bright gradients, cartoon mascots, feature-list overload. This programme is for students who want to do rigorous science — the design should match that seriousness.
- **Generic NGO / school brochure**: pastel backgrounds, soft round fonts, feel-good stock photography, diluted ambition. SRI is not a charity — it is a high-performance mentorship programme that also happens to include scholarships.

## Design Principles

1. **Earned authority over borrowed prestige.** Trust comes from specificity — real hours, real phases, real price points — not from logo badges or generic superlatives. Every slide should tell you something concrete.
2. **World-class ambition, Indian accessibility.** The visual register should feel ISEF-level serious while remaining legible to a teacher in any city. Aspiration and inclusion are not opposites.
3. **Show the pathway, don't just claim it.** The IRIS → ISEF journey must feel concrete and staged, not abstract. Design should make progress visible.
4. **Restraint as a signal.** The absence of coaching-centre noise IS the message. A quiet, precise deck tells a teacher this programme takes science seriously.
5. **Scholarship is core, not a footnote.** Full access for underprivileged students is part of the brand identity — not a CSR bullet point. It deserves equal visual weight.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as target. Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1. Large display headings ≥ 3:1. The deck renders at 1920×1080 for projection; type sizes are fixed-scale for that viewport. Reduced motion not currently implemented — a concern if the deck gains animation. The programme itself serves students from all economic backgrounds; the design should never visually signal exclusivity.
